## The useful brand asset: specific authorship

**Strategic inference:** The founder, individual artworks, and the transformation from painting to garment can give TCPF a concrete identity beyond the broad promise of contemporary Filipino clothing.

Art and Filipiniana already have a wider cultural relationship. CCP's 2025 Filipiniana x Obra program paired terno design with Philippine visual art. The brand can confidently foreground Tisha's own work without suggesting it invented the category. [CCP](https://culturalcenter.gov.ph/press-release/fashion-and-visual-arts-merge-in-ccps-filipiniana-x-obra-exhibit-at-the-national-museum-of-fine-ar/)

PhilSTAR Life's coverage of Tisha's Handuraw collection describes Mayon paintings and Pabalat references. This offers a credible research thread for the artist's regional and cultural imagination. It does not establish that those specific portfolio garments are part of the ready-to-wear range. [PhilSTAR Life](https://philstarlife.com/amp/article/335705-sinulid-filipino-fashion-revolution)

**Implication:** Introduce each confirmed artwork with a title, artist credit, source image, and concise story when those facts are available. Showing the real source art alongside its garment application could make authorship understandable in a few seconds.

## Competitive field

The [six-brand comparison](competitor-profiles/_summary.md) separates overlap from useful benchmarks.

- Filibela provides a clear wearability/rewear proposition and categories organized by garment and occasion.
- Mestiza Filipina makes contemporary Filipiniana legible through mini dresses, tops, coordinated bottoms, and other silhouettes.
- Kaayo and Filip + Inna ground their stories in identified craft traditions and artisan relationships.
- VINTA's current website emphasizes Philippine atelier services and distinguishes made-to-measure, custom, and couture.
- Kultura provides multibrand shopping breadth and visible material, model, and care information.

**Strategic inference:** TCPF can combine direct shopping clarity with a more explicit artwork story. This is an opportunity to investigate, not proof that the market is empty or competitors lack original art. The sampled competitor prices also concern different fabrics, construction, garment types, and sales conditions; they do not justify a TCPF price increase.

## Buying questions to carry into phase 2

1. **Which artwork am I choosing?** Its title, creator, inspiration, and how it appears on the garment.
2. **What exactly will arrive?** A top, bolero, dress, skirt, trousers, or a complete set; separate styling items must be clear.
3. **Will it fit?** Body versus garment measurements, available sizes, silhouette, closures, lining, stretch, and model information where verified.
4. **What will it feel and look like?** Actual material, transparency, print technique, color, construction, and care.
5. **Can it arrive before my occasion?** Stock versus made-to-order status and confirmed dispatch/delivery information.
6. **How can I style it?** Relevant coordinating products and accurate styling imagery.

These are candidate information needs, not a metafield schema. Prefer Shopify's standard product, variant, taxonomy, inventory, and shipping data where suitable. Consider reusable objects only for information that repeats or needs its own editorial treatment.

## Visual evidence and creative implications

The two inspected 2022 publication images show expressive landscape/floral art, pastel color alongside stronger blues and reds, and art printed across clothing. They support a colorful artistic vocabulary. They do not establish today's logo, palette, typography, or campaign style. [Reference images and provenance](research/source-register.md#research-image-provenance)

**Proposal:** Give the clothing and original art enough visual space to carry the identity. Use a controlled interface palette and expressive editorial details rather than adding a different decorative style to every section. Shopping controls should remain easy to recognise.

**Proposal:** Treat artwork storytelling as an explanation people can see: source painting, garment placement, and detail. A beautiful campaign image alone cannot answer fit, fabric, and set-inclusion questions.

## Brand voice implications

**Proposed starting voice:** Warm, assured, artistic, and specific. Use a little poetry when describing a painting; use concrete language when someone is choosing a size or checking delivery.

Use precise garment names and artist attribution. Explain unfamiliar garment terms when useful. Decide whether Filipino/Bicol language belongs in the identity with the client, then use it naturally and have translations reviewed. Do not infer a compulsory bilingual voice from the brand's location.

Terms such as original, printed, hand-painted, bespoke, made-to-order, and ready-to-wear should retain distinct meanings. Sustainability, small-batch, handwoven, exclusivity, and material claims need product or operational evidence.
