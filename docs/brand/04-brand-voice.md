# TCPF Basics — voice and copy guide

Version 1.0 · Voice and headline approved through the user's written-system review on 2026-10-05. Product specimens remain illustrative sample copy.

## Voice in one sentence

**An artist welcoming you into her world, with a shopkeeper's clarity about the clothing.**

Warm, expressive, assured, specific, and easy to understand. Keep emotion in artwork stories and editorial headlines; give purchase information directly. This follows the [selected strategy](03-brand-strategy.md), rather than claiming to reproduce an audited social-media voice.

## Voice principles

| Principle | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Say what the piece is | Name the garment and the relevant feature. | “A printed bolero with structured butterfly sleeves.” |
| Give art a specific story | Name the artwork and use an approved artist note. | “This print comes from Tisha's painting [approved artwork title].” |
| Invite personal expression | Leave room for the wearer's taste and styling. | “Wear it with a simple skirt, or pair it with its matching piece.” |
| Keep warmth unforced | Use natural sentences and direct invitations. | “Have a project in mind? Tell us what you're imagining.” |
| Make decisions easier | Explain measurements, contents, policies, and next steps. | “This listing includes the bolero only.” |

Bracketed variables above illustrate a writing pattern; they must be replaced with verified information before publication. Do not publish template variables as product copy.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Practical rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign headline | Expressive and concise | One idea; immediately explain the offer nearby. |
| Artwork story | Reflective and specific | Describe the actual subject/process using the artist's approved context. |
| Product description | Warm and informative | State garment, print, shape, contents, then styling. |
| Size/care/shipping | Plain and precise | Explain the action or fact without poetic language. |
| Cart and errors | Calm and helpful | Name the problem and the next available action. |
| Bespoke inquiry | Personal and realistic | Invite a brief; explain what follows using confirmed operations. |

## Approved business language versus draft copy

**Client-confirmed facts:** TCPF means Tisha Chavez Painted Fashion; Tisha Chavez owns and operates the boutique; the business is based in Naga City; original art is printed onto modern Filipiniana.

**Preferred editorial spelling:** “TCPF Basics” in prose, with the existing logo's lettering preserved in the asset. Use “Tisha Chavez Painted Fashion” on first mention in the founder story. This spelling standard is proposed for consistency.

**Recommended main headline:** “Original art. A new way to wear Filipiniana.”

**Supporting line for a real catalog:** “Discover modern Filipiniana featuring original paintings by Tisha Chavez, from Naga City.”

**Supporting line for the demonstration catalog:** “Explore a sample collection showing how artwork, garment details, and styling come together.”

The real-catalog line should not describe AI concept paintings as Tisha's work. Label concept imagery where the demonstration uses it.

## Copy specimens

### Ready-to-wear introduction

> Color, character, and a silhouette of your own. Explore modern Filipiniana with a story in every print.

Action: **Shop ready-to-wear**. Use **View collection** when the destination is a specific collection.

### Our Story introduction

> TCPF stands for Tisha Chavez Painted Fashion. Based in Naga City, Tisha brings her original paintings into modern Filipiniana through printed clothing. This is a place to discover the art, the pieces, and the person behind them.

This uses the client brief. Longer founder history should retain dates and distinguish early directly painted work from the current printed range. See the [source register](research/source-register.md).

### Bespoke introduction

> Something personal in mind? Tell us about your occasion and the piece you're imagining. Start a conversation with TCPF about a bespoke project.

Action: **Inquire about bespoke**. Before publication, add a confirmed explanation of available services, required notice, and how quotes work. Do not invent response times or promise to accept every project.

### Demonstration product specimen

**Sample product:** Amihan Printed Bolero.

> A cream bolero with structured butterfly sleeves and a soft botanical print in plum and periwinkle. Pair it with a simple skirt to let the print lead, or explore the matching sample outfit.

> Demonstration concept: the garment, artwork, and photography are AI-generated. Product specifications and availability have not been approved for sale.

“Amihan” is a proposed sample name, not a verified TCPF SKU. Fabric, lining, fastening, measurements, and care must be separately specified as sample data in phase 2.

## Writing rules

- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, and product information. Preserve official artwork and garment names.
- Keep sentences short enough to understand on first reading. A product introduction should usually fit within 40–70 words.
- Use “you” naturally. Use “we” for the shop. Use “I” only in an approved quote or note from Tisha.
- Explain a specialist clothing term at its first useful appearance. Do not call every garment a terno solely because it is Filipiniana.
- Write prices consistently in the store's locale; a proposed Philippine display is “₱950”. Use Shopify's money formatting in the eventual theme.
- Use Filipino or Bikol words where the client supplies authentic meaning or phrasing. English is the current working language, not a confirmed final localization decision.
- Avoid decorative urgency, unsupported superlatives, and invented customer praise.

## Phrase choices

| Vague or misleading | Better pattern |
| --- | --- |
| “A masterpiece of timeless elegance” | Name the silhouette, print, and intended styling. |
| “Hand-painted clothing” for a printed piece | “Printed with artwork by Tisha Chavez,” when attribution is confirmed. |
| “Crafted from the finest materials” | State the actual composition and construction. |
| “Perfect for every body” | Provide measurements and describe the verified fit. |
| “Celebrity-approved” | Credit a specific confirmed appearance with its source. |
| “Limited edition” without a production limit | Omit the claim; show actual availability. |

## Content handover checklist

Before a piece of copy becomes public, check its attribution, technique, product facts, sample status, action label, and destination. Read it aloud once for warmth and clarity. The goal is a consistent voice without turning every practical instruction into a brand slogan.
